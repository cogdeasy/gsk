GSK: Manish Varma, the SAP Estate, and the S/4HANA Migration

Prepared for Ciaran Deasy, Cognition AI · August 2026

This brief is preparation for the meeting with Manish Varma (manish.x.varma@gsk.com). It covers who Manish is and how ERP fits his remit, what GSK's SAP estate almost certainly looks like today, the S/4HANA migration GSK is already running (the "ERP Evolution" programme), and what that migration entails in practice — the workstreams where Cognition can be relevant.

Manish Varma: Role and Background

Manish Varma is Global Vice President & Enterprise Head of Data & Analytics at GSK, based in Philadelphia, in post since January 2022.1 His LinkedIn headline is telling: "Global Technology & AI Executive | CIO | CDTO | Driving Enterprise Growth through AI Innovation, Data Foundation & ERP Modernization." ERP modernization is explicitly part of how he frames his mandate — he owns the enterprise data foundation that an ERP migration both depends on and feeds.

His career is deeply ERP- and pharma-native. He was a Pharma ERP Data Warehouse Architect at Bristol-Myers Squibb (1999–2001), then spent ~15 years at Johnson & Johnson rising to VP of IT — J&J being one of the largest global SAP estates in life sciences. He was Chief Strategy Officer at ArisGlobal (drug-safety SaaS), then CEO of Mareana, an AI/data startup focused on pharma manufacturing and supply-chain intelligence — i.e., extracting value from exactly the kind of data locked in ERP and MES systems. Wharton MBA, IIT Kanpur engineering.1

His public writing emphasises that enterprise AI advantage comes from proprietary data and operating context, that "data is capital; if it isn't trusted and decision-ready, AI will fail," and that AI only creates value when it changes how work gets done.2 Expect him to evaluate any migration tooling through that lens: does it make GSK's core data decision-ready and does it change how the work is actually executed.

Practical implication: Manish is unlikely to be the day-to-day owner of the S/4HANA programme delivery (that sits with GSK Tech programme leadership under CDTO Shobie Ramakrishnan), but as enterprise head of Data & Analytics he owns the data foundation the migration must not break — master data, analytics continuity, and the AI agenda layered on top. He is a strong internal champion candidate for AI-assisted engineering on the programme.

GSK's Likely SAP Set-Up Today

GSK's current estate is well documented and unusually consolidated for its size:

  • A single global SAP ECC 6.0 core. GSK ran a decade-long "ERP unification" onto SAP ECC worldwide, completed through wave deployments across the 2010s. By 2021 its global ERP competency centre described a unified ECC instance — but process mining (Celonis) revealed ~28,000 process variants for a single sales-order process, i.e., a nominally unified system with enormous embedded process and custom-code complexity.3
  • Massive operational footprint on that core. ~37 manufacturing sites, 1.7bn packs of medicines and ~409m vaccine doses shipped in 2024, operations across 75+ countries, with ECC driving manufacturing, supply chain, finance, procurement (Ariba), intercompany profit tracking (ICT), and even label printing at 3,500+ printers via Loftware integrated to ECC.4
  • Heavily GxP-regulated. The ERP is a validated system under pharma GMP rules — every change requires computer-system validation, which is the single biggest cost multiplier versus a non-pharma migration.
  • Deadline pressure. Mainstream maintenance for ECC 6.0 ends in 2027 (extended support to 2030), which is the forcing function behind the current programme.5

Reasonable inference on surroundings: SAP BW/analytics feeding the enterprise data platform Manish owns, Ariba for procurement, a large bespoke ABAP layer (the 28k variants imply years of Z-code accretion), and hundreds of interfaces to MES, LIMS, serialization/track-and-trace, and logistics partners.

The Migration Already Underway: ERP Evolution

GSK is not deciding whether to migrate — it is mid-flight. GSK's "ERP Evolution" programme (also referenced internally as "ERP Together") is deploying S/4HANA globally in waves across Pharma and Vaccines: commercial markets, manufacturing sites, R&D, shared financial services, plus customers, suppliers and logistics partners. A "Wave 0" covers a representative subset first; programme secondments run to end-2027.6

Public staff profiles indicate the target is S/4HANA on RISE with SAP (SAP-managed cloud), and that the programme has completed Blueprint and Confirm phases and is currently in Realization — system sizing, application architecture, integration planning done; build, test, data migration and wave cutovers now in progress.7 GSK is hiring ERP Process Directors (Logistics, Planning), PMO/cutover leads, integration leads and data SMEs on fixed terms to December 2027 — a classic multi-year, multi-wave brownfield-plus-redesign programme.

What the Migration Entails

For an estate like GSK's, an ECC → S/4HANA migration decomposes into these workstreams:

  • Custom code remediation. Years of custom ABAP (Z-programs, user exits, enhancements) must be inventoried, assessed against S/4 simplifications (e.g., new data model: MATDOC, ACDOCA, business partner mandate), and either retired, remediated, or rebuilt as clean-core extensions on BTP. At GSK's scale this is tens of thousands of objects.
  • Process standardisation. The 28,000 sales-order variants are the real migration problem: each wave forces a decision to adopt the global template or justify a deviation. Process mining, fit-gap workshops, and template governance per market/site.
  • Data migration & master data cleansing. Material masters, BOMs, recipes, vendors/customers (business-partner conversion), open items, batch/serialization data — extracted, cleansed, mapped and loaded per wave, with reconciliation evidence. This is squarely Manish's territory: the migration is also the once-a-decade chance to make core data "decision-ready."
  • Interface re-pointing. Hundreds of integrations (MES, LIMS, Loftware labeling, Ariba, banks, 3PLs, EDI with customers/suppliers) re-built or re-tested per wave.
  • GxP validation & testing. Every remediated object, interface and process needs documented test evidence; regression suites run per wave and per release. Typically 30–40% of programme effort in pharma.
  • Cutover & hypercare per wave. Rehearsed cutovers (GSK is hiring dedicated cutover leads), stock/finance reconciliation, hypercare, then the next wave — repeated across ~37 sites and dozens of markets through 2027+.
  • Change management & training for tens of thousands of users, plus benefits tracking.

Where Cognition Fits

The labour-intensive, pattern-heavy middle of this programme is where Devin is strongest:

  • ABAP custom-code analysis and remediation at scale — triaging simplification-item hits, mechanically remediating deprecated patterns, migrating to clean-core/BTP extensions, with each change as a reviewable PR.
  • Test asset generation — building and maintaining automated regression suites and validation documentation drafts per wave, cutting the GxP testing burden.
  • Data migration engineering — writing and iterating extraction/cleansing/mapping pipelines and reconciliation reports; directly supports Manish's "trusted, decision-ready data" agenda.
  • Interface remediation — repetitive re-pointing and re-testing of hundreds of integrations per wave.
